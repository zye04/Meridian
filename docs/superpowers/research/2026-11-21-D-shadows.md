# Topic D: sun shadows for dense raster nature scenes (2026-11-21)

Meridian today: 5 cached cascades (20*4^i m radius, 2048^2, texel-snapped, 4-tap PCF), cascade 1 texels ~8-10 cm so a crown's dapple at 12-80 m goes blobby (G5); contact shadows are a binary 16-step jittered march; far trees are missing from raster ground shadows (G11, WIP `far-m-shadow2`). Baseline R2 says Chlorophyll's trees cast clear shadows, also in aerials.

Verification (2026-11-21, online): [verified] = page or PDF fetched this session, title/authors/year read; [snippet] = confirmed only through a search-result summary; "unverified" = could not be confirmed, treat as a lead. Every ms figure for Meridian is my own estimate (unverified, not measured); no source gave a cost figure for the techniques below except where quoted.

## 1. Sources, newest first

**MegaLights: Stochastic Direct Lighting in UE5 (Krzysztof Narkowicz, Tiago Costa; SIGGRAPH 2025 Advances)** [verified, PDF read]. Many-light stochastic sampling with ray-traced shadows, with an option to fall back to virtual shadow maps for chosen lights ("VSMRT": plausible soft shadows and contact hardening); notes VSM caching schemes can accumulate cost quickly. Not our problem (one sun); confirms VSM + SMRT contact hardening is Epic's current soft-shadow answer. 

**Bend Studio screen-space shadows (Graham Aldridge; Days Gone, PS4 2019; code and slides from the SIGGRAPH 2023 Sony Creators page)** [verified: Bend's page and blog fetched; they state Days Gone was the first title to use it for sun shadows and offer a slides zip and a code zip, no GitHub link. Search results quote the code as `bend_sss_gpu.h`/`bend_sss_cpu.h`, Apache-2.0, and Martin Mittring's post says Apache-2.0]. Solves: small-scale occlusion (grass, pebbles, leaf clusters) that shadow-map texels miss. How [snippet]: wavefronts of 64 pixels march along the ray toward the light; a pixel is shadowed when one of the preceding ~60 depths lies within ~0.5% of its depth. Ports exist (Godot fork, Skyrim Community Shaders, Aurora viewer, UE5 write-up) [snippet]. Cost: no figure verified (unverified: I recall sub-millisecond on PS4-class). The zip was not downloaded or read.  

**UE 5 Virtual Shadow Maps (Epic docs, UE 5.x; page undated, year unverified; Fortnite Chapter 4 tech blog, year unverified)** [verified for the docs; the Fortnite blog returned 403 and was not read, nothing below is taken from it]. Directional light = clipmap levels, each its own 16k^2 virtual map in 128^2 pages, each level doubling coverage, ~64 cm to 40 km; pages allocated only where depth buffer needs them; cached across frames, invalidated by moving geometry, and any WPO/PDO material (wind foliage) invalidates its pages every frame. Docs advise contact shadows or disabling WPO for distant foliage, and distance-field shadows beyond a distance for non-Nanite. Cost splits into Shadow Depths (geometry) and Shadow Projection (SMRT). Solves: uniform texel density tied to screen pixels, so near leaf shadows are crisp. Cost: no figure verified; the docs only say cost splits into depths and projection, and non-Nanite geometry is much more expensive. Not portable to our pipeline without page tables and a screen-driven page marking pass. 

**Shadow Map Ray Tracing, SMRT (UE5 VSM)** [verified for the docs' description: rays toward the light tested against the virtual shadow map, giving contact hardening; "4-8 samples work well" is from a search snippet of the cvar docs]. Applying it to Meridian's cascades is my adaptation. Needs a temporal/spatial filter. Cost: no figure verified. This is the closest published fit for "crisp near, soft far" with a cached map.

**Shadows Optimizations in Cyberpunk 2077 (Bartłomiej Dybisz, Michał Witanowski; SIGGRAPH 2021 Talks)** [verified, ACM history archive page]. Three shadow classes (cascaded, distant, local); real-time and preprocessed culling plus shadow map caching to cut CPU/GPU cost. The slide PDF was not read: vegetation handling and ms are unverified. 

**The Vegetation of Horizon Zero Dawn (Gilbert Sanders, Guerrilla, GDC 2018, "Between Tech and Art")** [verified, slides PDF read]. Sun shadows use four cascades: a 1024^2 compartment map (Aloy only), cascade 0 2048^2 to 8 m, cascade 1 2048^2 to 80 m, and a distant cascade that is "height-field-based", 1536^2 over a 1.5 x 1.5 km region around the camera, with smooth alpha-blended crossfades between cascades. Vegetation has separate shadow-casting meshes: S1 = LOD3 visual mesh, alpha-tested, animated, depth-only (cascades 0/1); S2 = fewer triangles, non-alpha-tested, non-animated. Our cascade 1 already matches Horizon's reach (80 m) at the same 2048^2, so Horizon's near shadows had comparable texels there. The height-field map is the pattern for G11. No ms figures in the slides. 

**Hashed Alpha Testing (Chris Wyman, Morgan McGuire; NVIDIA; 2017)** [verified, PDF read; venue (I3D) unverified, year from the NVIDIA URL path]. Stochastic/hashed alpha thresholds stop alpha-mapped geometry disappearing with distance, adding noise that TAA resolves. No cost figure verified; it is one hash per fragment. . Companion: "Exploring ways to mipmap alpha-tested textures" (lisyarus, 5 Jan 2025) [verified]; concludes averaging premultiplied colours and max-downsampling an SDF, not simple coverage scaling. . Whether Meridian's cook (ktx2.cpp coverage mips) matches the shadow pass's sampling is a to-check, not a finding.

**CSM Scrolling / CSM Caching (Mike Day, Al Hastings; presented by Mike Acton, Insomniac Games, SIGGRAPH 2012 Advances)** [verified, PDF read; year from the URL path]. Scroll the cached cascade map and render only the newly exposed strip (lateral and depth scrolling). Meridian re-renders whole cascades; scrolling could shrink the 40-51 ms first-frame/hitch cases in the backlog (inference, not measured). 

**PCSS (Fernando, NVIDIA, 2005)** [unverified: not fetched]. Blocker search + variable PCF; from my knowledge it handles thin multi-layer foliage poorly (averaged blocker depth over leaves and gaps).

**Moment / EVSM shadow maps** [unverified, from my own knowledge]. Filterable but light-bleeds on layered leaves and costs ~4x memory; not recommended.

**Far/terrain shadow sources**: "Optimizing Terrain Shadows" (Matthäus Chajdas, GPUOpen, 15 Dec 2016) [verified]: tiles whose triangles all face the sun are skipped in shadow maps, removing ~50% of terrain geometry in typical scenes. . "High-Quality Shadows for Streaming Terrain Rendering" (Chajdas, Reichl, Dick, Westermann; TU Munich; Eurographics 2015 short paper) [verified]: ray-cast near shadows plus precomputed far shadows at ~3 bit per height sample for a day-night cycle. . UE distance-field terrain shadows to km range [snippet, unverified]. I found no published voxel-based tree-shadow technique for open worlds; Meridian's brick far map is closest to Horizon's height-field distant cascade.

## 2. What Chlorophyll probably does

Baseline: technique unknown (R2: clear tree shadows, also far; R1: far trees lit as volumes with sun visibility). Best inference, with confidence:
- Near field uses a shadow-map or screen-space mix that is crisp at 1-30 m: medium confidence (frames show crisp ground dapple; method unknowable).
- Far trees cast shadows from a voxel representation (world bricks or per-tree voxels, marched toward the sun, or a map rasterized from them): medium-high, because his far trees are voxels and he has no impostor geometry to render into cascades.
- Contact detail from a screen-space march: low-medium; Bend SSS is the industry norm since 2023.
- No RT: low-medium (the baseline's RT assumption is ours; his own talks mention SS+voxel GI, per memory file).

## 3. Recommendations for Meridian (RTX 4080, 2560x1440; every ms figure is my unverified estimate, to be measured on the GPU pass table)

Ranked by value per risk.

**1. Port Bend SSS as the contact-shadow pass (G5).** Replace the 16-step jittered march with the wavefront march over the final depth, 2 passes if needed for tile borders. Keep the sun-radiance subtraction scheme (the HDR-subtract design stays) but feed it the Bend mask. Expect ~0.2-0.4 ms at 1440p versus the current pass (measure first; the current pass is a similar order). Gains: longer, cleaner rays, no per-pixel jitter. Risk: thin foliage depth gives wrong or missing occluders (use the thin-occluder tolerance, disable on masked grass if over-dark). Verify with the motion gates, not just stills. Apache-2.0 per search results and Mittring's post [snippet]; download the zip from bendstudio.com/siggraph and confirm the licence header first.

**2. Fix far tree shadows by sampling a transmittance far map, not first-hit depth (G11).** Current far map stores depth of the first 0.5-opacity hit at 8 m texels; at km range that gives hard 8 m blobs. Option: store sun optical depth (Beer, like the existing cloud shadow map) per texel and read exp(-tau), blended in with cascade 4 beyond cascade 3's range; this also softens naturally with distance and avoids the sharp-versus-missing mismatch. Horizon uses a height-field distance cascade, same idea. Cost: build is already budgeted (slice per frame); a read is one fetch, ~0.1 ms. Needs the `far-m-shadow2` branch plus Debug checks. Highest impact on aerial frames (R2).

**3. Cascade 1 resolution/ratio (G5, G11 cause row 38).** Cheapest immediate fix: 4096^2 for cascade 1 (and maybe 0 stays 2048): texels 7.8 to 3.9 cm, 32 MB D32, re-render cost grows only when cascade 1 scrolls. Or ratio 3 (20,60,180,540,1620,4860 m, 6 cascades) at same total memory +1 map; texel at 60-180 m drops about 25%. Pair with CSM scrolling (Acton) to cut re-render hitches (47-51 ms first frames in the backlog). Expected steady-state cost: ~0 on cached frames; scroll strips 0.3-1 ms spikes.

**4. Contact-hardening via SMRT-style sampling in `cascadeVisibility` (G5).** Replace the fixed 3x3 tent with 4-8 taps along rays to a point on the sun disk, depth-compared in the finest cascade, penumbra from the blocker distance at the first hit (receiver-plane bias retained). Better than PCSS for foliage because it ray-walks rather than averaging blocker depth. Needs blue-noise per pixel and TAA to resolve; the backlog notes a time-fixed jitter left static dither and a fully time-varying one adds shimmer, so budget a motion-gate pass. Cost: ~0.3-0.8 ms at 1440p (about 1 map fetch per sample; 4 samples roughly doubles the current 4-tap cost). Do after 1 and 3 so you can tell which fixed the blobbiness.

**5. Alpha-tested shadow quality.** In shadow_masked.frag use the same mip and a hashed threshold (Wyman) so leaves keep coverage in cascades 2-4. ~0 ms. Read shadow_masked.frag first; part of it may exist.

**6. Far switch per cascade texel (backlog row 38).** Put far-field voxel trees or their proxy into cascades 3-4 instead of cluster geometry (Horizon assigns casters to cascades by fixed distance). Targets the ~39 ms first-frame re-render at the forest edge; pairs with item 2.

**Not recommended**: EVSM/moment maps (light bleeding on layered leaves), full UE-style virtual shadow maps (page tables and screen-driven marking, too large for one light), PCSS with blocker averaging, RT shadows as default.

## 4. Order

1 -> 2 -> 3 (with scrolling) -> 5 -> 4 -> 6. Gate each on `tools/motion-gates.sh`, the forest overview view and the GPU pass table; log the choice in `docs/decisions.md`.
